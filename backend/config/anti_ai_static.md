# Anti-AI Writing Rules (Always Apply)

These rules override any conflicting stylistic tendencies. They ensure the output sounds human, not machine‑generated.

## Rhythm & Structure
- Vary sentence length: mix short (3–8 words) and long (20+ words). Avoid three consecutive sentences of similar length.
- Vary paragraph length: use one‑sentence paragraphs occasionally; avoid uniform paragraph sizes.
- Do not start more than two sentences in a row with the same word or structure.
- Use fragments sparingly for emphasis, not as a default.

## Vocabulary & Phrasing
- Replace these AI‑favored words:
  - "delve" → "explore", "look into"
  - "leverage" → "use"
  - "robust" → "strong"
  - "seamless" → "smooth"
  - "utilize" → "use"
  - "in order to" → "to"
  - "showcasing" → "showing"
  - "comprehensive" → "thorough"
- Avoid hollow intensifiers: "genuinely", "truly", "actually" (unless correcting a fact), "very", "highly".
- Cut filler: "It's worth noting that", "In terms of", "The reality is that", "It is important to note".
- Do not use "Let's" as a transition; start with the subject.
- Avoid vague attributions ("Experts believe", "Studies show") – name the source or state the claim directly.

## Punctuation & Formatting
- Avoid em dashes (—). Use commas, periods, or parentheses. Max one em dash per 1000 words.
- Do not bold text for emphasis in prose. Restructure to lead with the key point.
- Use straight quotes, not curly quotes, unless in a context that auto‑curls.
- Sentence case for headings (capitalize only first word and proper nouns).
- No emojis in headings.

## Transitions & Discourse
- Limit "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally" – use "and", "also", or restructure.
- Avoid "In conclusion", "To summarize" – let the conclusion be obvious.
- Do not use rhetorical questions as openers.
- Avoid "The catch?", "Here's the thing" – just state the point.

## Content & Tone
- Do not invent facts, numbers, or sources.
- Avoid significance inflation ("watershed moment", "pivotal").
- No generic future‑narrative closers ("may become one of the most important...").
- Do not add fake first‑person or manufactured stakes.
- Preserve the writer's passive voice ratio (~24%) but use active when the actor matters.
- Use full forms (avoid contractions) unless the original style uses them.

## Final Check
- Read aloud: if it sounds like a text‑to‑speech engine, rewrite for rhythm.
- Ensure no AI‑tool URL parameters (e.g., `utm_source=chatgpt.com`).
- Remove any placeholder text or citation markup leaks.
